## Why HR, and why AI

Fourteen years in HR taught me where organizational problems actually live: in process, incentive and data that nobody has cleaned up. Workforce analytics, HR system optimization, organizational redesign — the discipline is the same as information systems management: understand the business process first, then change the system that supports it.

What I could not do was build. So I learned — low-code first, then Python — and shipped two products on my own initiative. My gap is engineering depth, not domain judgment. That is precisely what I am applying to close.

## Two failures worth reading

- **A 55.6% hit rate is not a result.** My first backtest looked respectable until I understood that 18 signals prove nothing. The write-up in `market-thermometer` documents why, and what a valid test requires.
- **A 15-sample validation set.** CapitalTrack's validation was too small to support any conclusion. I kept the number in the repository rather than removing it.

I would rather be accurate about what I have not proven than impressive about what I have.

## Working method

Everything I build follows the same division of labour:

- **Design and judgement: mine.** What the product should do, how it is scored, what counts as evidence, and when to change direction.
- **Implementation: AI-assisted, human-directed.** Coze workflows are assembled by me on the platform; front-end and Python code are drafted with AI assistance, then specified, verified and iterated by me.
- **Documentation: continuous.** Every product keeps a build log, including the mistakes.

I have no formal programming background. I state that plainly — not as a caveat, but because closing that gap is the reason I am applying. What I bring is fourteen years of reading a business process and knowing which part of it is worth rebuilding.
